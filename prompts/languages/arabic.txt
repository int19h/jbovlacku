Language rules: Arabic

Choose a Modern Standard Arabic word, not a dialect word: for bread, خُبْز, not عَيْش.
Work from the fully vowelled form. Identify the root and the pattern, but cut only the parts listed below. Never cut the word down to its root.
Write word and stem in the same style: both with full vowel marks, or both without them.

Choose the form.
- For a verb, choose the perfect third-person masculine singular: كَتَبَ, not يَكْتُبُ.
- Choose the singular, never a plural. A broken plural has no removable ending: choose كِتَاب, not كُتُب.
- Choose the verbal noun when the target is a process or property. Choose the verb when the target is an action that x1 deliberately does to x2. Say which in note. For teaching as a process, تَعْلِيم stays whole. For the act of a teacher on a pupil, عَلَّمَ gives عَلَّم.
- For a property, choose its noun: for ease, سُهُولَة, not the adjective سَهْل.

Remove these parts.
- The definite article ال: الكتاب gives كتاب, الشمس gives شمس. Remove ال only when it is the article. أَلَم, "pain", stays أَلَم.
- Case endings and nunation, including the alif of accusative nunation: كِتَابٌ gives كِتَاب, كِتَابًا gives كِتَاب, بَيْتِ gives بَيْت.
- Person endings on a verb: كَتَبَ gives كَتَب, عَقَّمَ gives عَقَّم, كَتَبَتْ and كَتَبُوا give كَتَب. In a hollow verb, the long vowel is part of the root and stays: قَالَ gives قَال.
- Attached pronoun suffixes: كِتَابِي gives كِتَاب, سَيَّارَتِي gives سَيَّارَة.
- Sound plural endings -ون/-ين/-ات and the dual, if a plural was given anyway: مُعَلِّمُون and مُعَلِّمِينَ give مُعَلِّم, كِتَابَان gives كِتَاب.
- Feminine agreement ة on an adjective or participle, when the masculine form carries the same meaning: كَبِيرَة gives كَبِير, مَكْتُوبَة gives مَكْتُوب. Keep ة when the word with ة is a noun with its own meaning: تُفَّاحَة, "an apple", stays تُفَّاحَة.
- The nisba ending -ِيّ/-ِيَّة of an adjective, when the target is the noun relation: تَارِيخِيّ gives تَارِيخ, عِلْمِيّ gives عِلْم. -ِيَّة that makes the abstract noun of a property or a noun goes too: حُرِّيَّة gives حُرّ, إِنْسَانِيَّة gives إِنْسَان. Keep -ِيَّة when the noun names a different thing: جَمْعِيَّة, an association, stays whole. -ِيَّة is a short [i] and a doubled [jj]: حُرِّيَّة is [ħurˈrijja].

Keep these parts.
- Every pattern vowel and every derivational consonant of the chosen form. مَدْرَسَة stays مَدْرَسَة, never درس. مَكْتَب stays مَكْتَب, never كتب.
- The ة that belongs to a noun pattern. It is part of the pattern, not an ending: مُفَاعَلَة as in معايرة, فُعُولَة as in سُهُولَة [suˈhuːla], تَفْعِلَة as in تَجْرِبَة [ˈtadʒriba], and from a root that ends in a weak letter, with a short i and a consonant y: تَرْبِيَة [ˈtarbija], تَوْصِيَة [ˈtawsˤija], فَعَالَة as in نَظَافَة [naˈðˤaːfa]. All of them stay whole.
- All the material of a derived form, II through X: a doubled middle consonant, a long ā, and a prefix such as أَ, تَ, اِنْ or اِسْتَ, and the inserted تَ of Form VIII. تَعْقِيم, تَعَوُّد, تَبَخُّر, اِجْتِمَاع and اِسْتِخْدَام stay whole, because the pattern carries the causative, reflexive or process meaning.
- The verbal noun تَفْعِيل of a Form II verb has no doubled consonant, although the verb has one: عَقَّمَ, but تَعْقِيم [taʕˈqiːm]. عَلَّمَ, but تَعْلِيم [taʕˈliːm].

Pronunciation.
Use Modern Standard Arabic without case endings or nunation. Transcribe ة at the end of a word as a, with no [h]: مَدْرَسَة is [ˈmadrasa], مَعْرِفَة is [ˈmaʕrifa].
After removing ال, transcribe the stem as a word of its own, without the assimilation to the article: الشَّمْس has word_ipa [ʔaʃˈʃams], and the stem شَمْس has ipa [ʃams].
Write ج as [dʒ] in word_ipa and ipa: جَبَل is [ˈdʒabal].
In word_ipa, stress the last syllable if it has a long vowel and a consonant, or two consonants: كِتَاب [kiˈtaːb]. Otherwise stress the second-last syllable if it has a long vowel or ends in a consonant: مُعَلِّم [muˈʕallim]. Otherwise stress the third-last: مَدْرَسَة [ˈmadrasa].

Scoring.
Write ع [ʕ] as nothing in every position, and keep the vowels on both sides. Say in note that it was dropped. عَمَل gives amal. سَاعَة [ˈsaːʕa] gives saa. مَعْرِفَة gives marifa.
Write ء and any glottal stop [ʔ] as nothing, whatever letter carries it, and keep the vowels around it. رَأْس [raʔs] gives ras. سُؤَال [suˈʔaːl] gives sual.
Write ح [ħ] and خ [x] as x: حَرْب gives xarb, خُبْز gives xubz. Write غ [ɣ] as g: غَرْب gives garb, لُغَة gives luga. Write ق [q] as k: قَلَم gives kalam, قَمَر gives kamar. Write ه [h] as x: نَهْر gives naxr, هَرَم gives xaram.
Write ث as s: ثَلْج gives saldj. Write ذ as z: ذَهَب gives zaxab. Write ص as s: صَبْر gives sabr. Write ض as d: ضَوْء gives dau. Write ط as t: طِفْل gives tifl. Write ظ as z: نَظَافَة gives nazafa. Write ش as c: شَمْس gives cams. Write ج as dj: جَبَل gives djabal.
Write consonantal ي [j] as i and consonantal و [w] as u: يَوْم gives iaum, وَرَق gives uarak.
Write a long vowel once and a geminate consonant once: كِتَاب gives kitab, عَلَّم gives alam. Alif maqsura ى is a long a: هُدَى gives xuda. Write short a i u as a i u.

This call covers one language and one target meaning.
The output feeds a Lojban word builder. The builder needs a short sound sequence that carries the target meaning in this language.
Follow the steps in order. The sound guide and the language block below give the exact rules for this language.

Step 1. Choose the word.
Choose the one word that a fluent speaker of this language uses for the target meaning in ordinary or technical speech.
Prefer the word with the exact meaning over a word with a broader or nearby meaning.
A borrowed word is as good as a native word. Never prefer a native word with a broader meaning over a borrowed word with the exact meaning.
Example: for calibration, Malay kalibrasi is right and keyakinan ("confidence") is wrong.
Example: for calibration, Hindi कैलिब्रेशन and अंशांकन are both right. Choose one of them. विश्वास ("confidence") is wrong.
Example: for sterilization, Spanish esterilización is right and limpieza ("cleaning") is wrong.
Use normal native spelling. Use one word, not a phrase. Do not join the words of a phrase into one word. A word joined by hyphens counts as one word: French arc-en-ciel is one word. French pomme de terre is a phrase.
Do not choose a less suitable word because its sounds are easier to convert.
If the language has no single word for the meaning, set word to null and explain in note.
Example: English has no single word for the day after tomorrow. Japanese has 明後日, and German has übermorgen.

Step 2. Write the selected sense.
Write in English the meaning of the chosen word that you selected, in one sentence.
If that sense is broader or narrower than the target, say so in note in one clause. Other senses of the same word are irrelevant.
Example: for nephew, Hindi भतीजा has the sense "a brother's son". The note says that it is narrower, because a sister's son is भांजा.

Step 3. Segment the word.
Split the word into its base and its attached parts, using the language block.
A matching letter sequence is not a part. -ion in calibration is a part, because calibrate exists beside calibration. -ate in plate is not a part, because no word pl- carries the meaning.
-er in teacher is a part, because teach is the base. -er in water is not a part, because no word wat- carries the meaning.

Step 4. Remove the parts that the language block lists as removable.
Remove inflection: case, number, gender agreement, person, tense, mood, infinitive endings, definite articles. Example: German Hunde gives Hund. Spanish cantamos gives cant.
Remove category-changing material: endings or affixes that turn a verb into a noun, a noun into a verb, an adjective into a noun, or a noun into an adjective. Example: darkness gives dark. childish gives child.
Remove phase material: affixes that mark only the start, the completion, the result, or the aspect of the same relation. Example: Russian сделать gives дел, because с- only marks the perfective. German aufessen gives ess, because auf- only marks that the eating is complete.
For this task, the verb, the action noun, the property noun, and the adjective of one relation all count as the same relation. Calibrate, calibration, and calibrated are one relation. Elastic and elasticity are one relation. Habituate and habituation are one relation. Removing the ending that moves between these forms never loses the relation.
Keep material that adds a required role or a required meaning: causation ("make sterile" against "be sterile"), agency, negation, capability, a prefix that changes the meaning (German ver-, Russian ис-), and the second half of a compound.
Example of negation: unhappy keeps un-, because happy has the opposite meaning. Example of capability: readable keeps -able, because read alone does not say that something can be read.
Agency means an affix that adds a doer to a relation that had none. An agent-noun suffix only names x1 of the same relation, so it goes: teacher gives teach.
Example: German Handschuh, "glove", stays Handschuh, because Schuh alone means shoe. Fahrrad, "bicycle", stays Fahrrad, because Rad alone means wheel. Spanish girasol, "sunflower", stays girasol, not sol.
Example: sterilize keeps -ize, because the target is deliberately treating an object, and steril- only names the resulting state. colonize keeps -ize, because the target is making a place into a colony.
Example: Russian привыкание becomes привык. The action-noun -ание goes. The imperfective theme -а- goes, because the perfective base привык is attested and carries the same relation. The prefix при- stays, because вык- alone has no such meaning.
Example: German Verdunstung becomes verdunst. The -ung goes. The ver- stays, because Dunst alone means haze.
Keep removing until no removable part remains. Example: German Freiheiten loses the plural -en and then -heit, and gives frei. If one removal is certain and a further removal is uncertain, make the certain removal and keep the rest. Explain the uncertainty in note.
Keep the whole word only when it contains no removable part. Then say so in note. Segment a loanword by the morphology of the language that uses it: Malay kalibrasi stays whole, because Malay has no base kalibr-. Example: English sugar has no removable part, so the stem is sugar. Difficulty, length, uncertainty, and the word being a loan or a compound are not reasons to keep the whole word.
Do not change the target definition to justify a cut or a retention.

Step 5. Write the retained form in stem, in native spelling.
The stem is a piece of the chosen word. It may be bound and unable to stand alone.
Do not write a different word in stem. If élasticité is the word, the stem is élastic, not élastique. If Bewegung is the word, the stem is beweg, not bewegen. If Spanish capacidad is the word, the stem is capac, not capaz. If the better choice is another word, change word and start again from step 2.
A language block can tell you to write the base spelling in stem instead, as in English and Malay. Then follow the language block.
Name any base alternation that you used in note, such as calibration to calibrate, or elasticity to elastic.
If you cannot identify any defensible stem, leave stem, ipa and sounds null, keep word and sense, and say in note what you do not know.

Step 6. Write the pronunciation.
First write in word_ipa the pronunciation of the whole chosen word, with ˈ before its stressed syllable. If the language has no word stress, as in Japanese and Mandarin, write no stress mark, and do not write tone or pitch marks.
Put ˈ before the whole stressed syllable, with all of its first consonants: calibration is [ˌkælɪˈbreɪʃən], not [ˌkælɪbˈreɪʃən]. décision is [desiˈzjɔ̃], not [desizˈjɔ̃].
Then write in ipa the part of that pronunciation that belongs to the stem, nothing more and nothing less.
Keep each sound of the stem as it sounds inside the chosen word, with one exception. A sound at the cut that changed only because of the removed ending takes the shape it has in the base before a vowel. This covers final devoicing, palatalization, velar softening (c as [s] before i), and a consonant fused with a removed ending (t as [ʃ] before -ion). Name the form that shows that shape in note, such as élastique or calibrate.
Example: English action is [ˈækʃən]. Its stem act has ipa [ækt], because the [t] fused with -ion as [ʃ]. Its sounds are akt.
Example: German Erlebnis is [ɛɐ̯ˈleːpnɪs]. Its stem erleb has ipa [ɛɐ̯leːb], as in erleben, because the b is [p] only before -nis. Its sounds are erleb.
Reduced vowels follow the stress of the whole word. Keep them as they are pronounced in the chosen word. Example: political is [pəˈlɪtɪkəl], so its stem politic is [pəlɪtɪk], with the reduced first vowel of political. atomic is [əˈtɑmɪk], so its stem atom is [ətɑm], not the [ˈætəm] of atom alone. The scoring step may restore them. The ipa field never does.
A stress mark in ipa does not matter. Keep it if it falls inside the stem. If the stressed syllable was cut off, ipa has no stress mark.
Do not mark syllable breaks.
If you do not know the pronunciation, leave word_ipa, ipa and sounds null and keep the earlier fields.

Step 7. Convert ipa to sounds with the sound guide.
Work through the ipa symbol by symbol. Write one scoring letter or letter pair for each sound, or nothing for a sound the guide says to drop.
The language block may tell you to write a reduced vowel as the vowel of a stressed form. Apply that in sounds only, and name the stressed form in note.
Write a long vowel once. Write a long consonant once. Write an affricate as stop plus fricative: tc, dj, ts, dz, pf. Write a glide as i or u.
The tool rejects any other character in sounds.
The scoring alphabet is absolute. sounds MUST contain only these letters: a b c d e f g i j k l m n o p r s t u v x z.
sounds MUST NOT contain h, q, w, y, an apostrophe, a space, a digit, an IPA symbol, or any accented letter.
This rule has no exception. If a sound has no letter in that alphabet, use the approximation the guide gives for it, or drop it where the guide says to drop it.

Step 8. Check the chain before you answer.
Every consonant and vowel in ipa has a letter in sounds, unless the guide drops that sound. Count the rows of the guide, not the letters. One sound can give two letters, such as [tʃ] tc, [eɪ] ei or [ɲ] ni.
No letter in sounds lacks a sound in ipa.
The stem is a piece of word, or the base spelling where the language block asks for it. word_ipa is the pronunciation of word. ipa is the part of word_ipa that belongs to stem, with only the sound at the cut changed as step 6 allows. The sense matches word.
The note is one to three sentences in English. It names: a sense difference from the target, a missing word or stem and why, each removed part, a whole word that was kept and why, an uncertain removal, any base alternation, any sound restored at the cut and the form that shows it, any restored vowel and its stressed form, any dropped or approximated sound. Nothing else. Do not leave draft corrections in note. If nothing needs explaining, note is an empty string.
Use null for an unknown word, sense, stem, word_ipa, ipa, or sounds. Never give stem without word, ipa without stem or word_ipa, or sounds without ipa.
Return sounds as one string of 1 through 64 scoring letters with no spaces or separators, such as kalibreit.

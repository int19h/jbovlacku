Sound guide: IPA to Lojban scoring letters.
The scoring letters are these Lojban letters: a b c d e f g i j k l m n o p r s t u v x z. No other letter is a scoring letter.
Each row below gives IPA sounds on the left and the scoring letter or letters on the right. Square brackets enclose IPA.
The same row applies in every language, unless the language block gives another rule for that sound.
Use the row, not the shape of the symbol. Some IPA symbols look like scoring letters but have other values: IPA [j] is i, IPA [c] is k, IPA [y] is i. The scoring letter j is the sound [ʒ], and the scoring letter c is the sound [ʃ].

Vowels
[a], [ɑ], [ɐ], [ʌ], [ä] : a
[æ] : a
[e], [ɛ], [ə], [ɜ], [ɘ], [ø], [œ] : e
[i], [ɪ], [ɨ], [y] : i
[o], [ɔ], [ɤ], [ɵ] : o
[u], [ʊ], [ɯ], [ʉ] : u
[ɒ] : o
A long vowel is written once. [aː] : a.
A diphthong is written as two vowels: [eɪ] ei, [aɪ] ai, [ɔɪ] oi, [aʊ] au, [oʊ] ou, [ei] ei.
A nasal vowel is written as its oral vowel. Add n only when a nasal consonant is pronounced. [ɛ̃] : e. [ɐ̃w̃] : au. Polish ręka [ˈrɛŋka] gives renka, because [ŋ] is pronounced. Polish są [sɔ̃w̃] gives sou.
A devoiced vowel that is still present is written as its vowel: Japanese 好き [sɯ̥ki] gives suki.
Where a language block says to write a reduced vowel from a stressed form, that rule wins in sounds.

Glides
[j], including the glide at the start of a rising diphthong such as [ja], [jo], [ju] : i
[w], [ʍ], including the glide at the start of [wa], [wo], [wi] : u
The vowel after the glide keeps its own row: [ja] gives ia, [wo] gives uo.
[ɥ] : i
Secondary palatalization, written as a small ʲ after a consonant, is not a glide. Write nothing for it. [rʲ] : r. [lʲ] : l. Russian мясо [ˈmʲasə] gives mase.
A separate palatal glide after a consonant is a glide. Japanese きゃ [kja] : kia. Japanese りょ [ɾjo] : rio.

Stops
[p], [pʰ], [p̚] : p
[b] : b
[t], [tʰ], [t̪], [ʈ], [ʈʰ], [t̚] : t
[d], [d̪], [ɖ], [dʱ] : d
[k], [kʰ], [c], [q], [k̚] : k
[ɡ], [ɢ], [ɟ], [ɡʱ] : g
An unaspirated voiceless stop is p, t, k. Mandarin b d g are [p t k] and are written p t k. Never write b, d, g for a voiceless sound.
[ʔ] : nothing
A long or geminate consonant is written once. [kː], [kk] : k.

Fricatives
[f], [ɸ] : f
[v], [β], [ʋ] : v
[s], [s̪] : s
[z] : z
[ʃ], [ʂ], [ɕ], [ç] : c
[ʒ], [ʐ], [ʑ] : j
[x], [χ], [ħ], [h], [ɦ] : x
[ɣ] : g
[θ] : s
[ð] : z
[ʕ] : nothing. Say in note that the pharyngeal was dropped.
[h] is x. Do not drop it, unless the language block says that h is silent.

Affricates
[tʃ], [tɕ], [ʈʂ], [tʃʰ], [tɕʰ], [ʈʂʰ] : tc
[dʒ], [dʑ], [ɖʐ] : dj
[ts], [tsʰ] : ts
[dz] : dz
[pf] : pf
Never reduce an affricate to one letter. A tie bar does not change the row: [t͡ʃ] is tc.

Nasals
[m], [ɱ] : m
[n], [n̪], [ɳ], [ɲ], [ŋ], [ɴ] : n
[ɲ] in a language that pronounces an audible glide after it, such as Malay ny before a vowel, : ni.
A syllabic nasal is written as the nasal alone, with no added vowel: German Garten [ˈɡaʁtn̩] gives gartn.

Liquids
[r], [ɾ], [ɹ], [ɻ], [ɽ], [ʀ], [ʁ] as a rhotic, and a non-syllabic vocalized r such as German [ɐ̯] : r
A syllabic [ɐ] is a vowel and gives a: Portuguese casa [ˈkazɐ] gives kaza.
[l], [ɫ], [ɭ], [ʎ], [ɬ], [ɮ] : l
[ʎ] in a language that pronounces an audible glide after it : li.
A syllabic liquid is written as the liquid alone: Czech vlk [vl̩k] gives vlk.

Marks
Stress, tone, length, syllable breaks, and breathy or creaky voice are not written.
Aspiration is not written. Retroflexion is not written. Dentalization is not written. Pharyngealization is not written.
Every other symbol in ipa must map to one of the rows above or to a scoring rule in the language block. If a symbol is in neither, choose the nearest row by place and manner, and name that choice in note.

The language block comes after this guide. Where its rule for a sound differs from a row of this guide, the language block wins for that language.
